\begin{abstract}
\MissingWork{ABSTRACT}{Principal findings and the final conclusion, after the
numerical experiments have been agreed and evaluated. The text below is a
provisional account of the problem and approach.}

High-order discontinuous Galerkin methods provide local approximations of
solutions to hyperbolic conservation laws, but unresolved discontinuities can
produce spurious oscillations. This thesis investigates projection-based
oscillation damping and its implementation on static curvilinear meshes.
The approach combines an adapted oscillation-free discontinuous Galerkin
(OFDG) filter with optional KXRCF troubled-cell indication, separating the
strength of damping from the decision of where to apply it.

The formulation acts on finite element functions independently of their
coefficient basis. Physical $L^2$ projections separate nonconstant components
for damping while retaining cell averages. On curved elements, first physical
derivatives are projected into the solution space and applied recursively to
construct the derivative sensor. These operations represent successively
projected derivatives rather than exact higher physical derivatives.
The methodological account distinguishes published formulations from the
adaptations used here and specifies the geometric and numerical assumptions
needed for their realization in MFEM.
\end{abstract}
