\chapter{Introduction}

High-order discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods combine element-local polynomial
approximations with numerical fluxes at element interfaces.  Their locality is
attractive for parallel computing, but high-order polynomials can develop
nonphysical oscillations near discontinuities.  Oscillation-free DG methods
address this by damping selected nonconstant modes while preserving cell
averages~\cite{LuEtAl2021,LiuEtAl2022,PengEtAl2024}.

The goal of this work is not only to reproduce a mathematical construction, but
to integrate it into MFEM in a form that remains understandable, testable, and
useful for several conservation laws.  Four practical questions drive the
implementation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Which face propagation speed should scale the OFDG sensor for nonlinear
        systems such as Euler?
  \item Can a troubled-cell indicator avoid unnecessary damping in smooth
        elements?
  \item How should OFDG and the indicator exchange face data on a distributed
        mesh?
  \item How does the adapted method compare with conventional RKDG and the
        scale-invariant 2024 OEDG method under reproducible accuracy,
        robustness, and cost measurements?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work and research gap}

The RKDG framework combines element-local DG spatial discretizations with
strong-stability-preserving explicit Runge--Kutta methods
\cite{CockburnShu1998,GottliebShu1998}.  Near discontinuities, nonlinear
limiters commonly use a troubled-cell indicator to avoid modifying smooth
regions.  KXRCF uses DG interface superconvergence for this purpose
\cite{KrivodonovaEtAl2004}; systematic comparisons and later alternatives show
that detector selectivity remains sensitive to normalization, polynomial
degree, and the chosen indicator variable
\cite{QiuShu2005,FuShu2017,VuikRyan2014,VuikRyan2016}.

OFDG instead adds projection-based damping whose strength is inferred from
interelement jumps~\cite{LuEtAl2021,LiuEtAl2022}.  The 2024 OEDG method recasts
that damping as exact modal attenuation and introduces scale- and
evolution-invariant coefficients~\cite{PengEtAl2024}.  The unresolved practical
question addressed here is how these ingredients behave when implemented in a
general finite element library, combined with selective KXRCF activation, and
extended across distributed meshes.  The combined implementation is not one of
the published methods verbatim; its deviations and empirical validation must
therefore be stated explicitly.

\section{Contributions}

The first contribution is a publicly accessible, reusable MFEM realization of
adapted OFDG and a fixed 2024 OEDG comparator.  Its polynomial-shell damping is
assembled through $L^2$ projection matrices in the finite-element basis supplied
by MFEM.  The represented solution therefore does not need to use modal,
orthogonal, or Legendre coefficients.  The same construction can be transferred
to another polynomial DG framework using its standard basis evaluation,
first-derivative, quadrature, transformation, and degree-of-freedom operations.
Higher derivatives required by the sensors are generated internally by repeated
application of first-derivative coefficient operators.

The second contribution is the coupling of adapted OFDG to a multidimensional,
multicomponent KXRCF activation mask.  An inactive element is copied unchanged
by the damping step, recovering the unfiltered DG update there and avoiding
unnecessary smooth-region attenuation.  Whether this selectivity improves
global error or end-to-end runtime is a question for the final measurements,
not an assumption of the method.

The software contribution further includes a shared face-physics interface,
Euler normal spectral radii $|\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|+c$, cached
element and face operators, MPI shared-face processing, conservative Euler
positivity limiting, and reusable MFEM example drivers.  Tests cover frozen
numerical fingerprints, conservation, vector orderings, multiple polynomial
bases, affine triangle and quadrilateral geometries, analytical wave speeds,
scale invariance, admissibility, and a discontinuity located exactly on a
two-rank partition boundary.  A manifest-driven pipeline stores raw outputs and
profiles, computes derived metrics, and generates the report figures and tables
without manual transcription.
