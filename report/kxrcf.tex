\section{KXRCF troubled-cell indication}
\label{chap:kxrcf}
A troubled-cell indicator decides where a separate stabilization should act;
it does not itself change the solution. The KXRCF indicator, named after
Krivodonova, Xin, Remacle, Chevaugeon, and Flaherty, supplies that decision in
this implementation. Adapted OFDG then damps the selected elements using the
operator in \cref{chap:ofdg}.

\subsection{Inflow jumps and normalization}
Let $K$ have outward unit normal $\boldsymbol n_K$ and neighbour $N$. Define
$[u_h]_{K,N}=u_h|_K-u_h|_N$. The part of the interior boundary on which
transport enters $K$ is identified by the signed normal transport velocity
$v_n$. For a prescribed, single-valued advection velocity $\boldsymbol a$,
this is $v_n=\boldsymbol a\cdot\boldsymbol n_K$: negative values mean inflow,
positive values mean outflow. The choices for two-sided velocities and
nonlinear systems are specified in \cref{sec:kxrcf-transport}. Define
\begin{equation}
 \partial K^- =\{x\in\partial K\text{ on interior faces}:
                         v_n(x)<0\}.
 \label{eq:kxrcf-inflow}
\end{equation}
\Cref{fig:kxrcf-inflow} shows which face enters this integral in a simple
rightward-flow example.

The indicator combines a signed jump integral with three normalization
factors. The physical measure $|\partial K^-|$ turns that integral into an
inflow average. The radius $r_K$ measures element size: it is half the cell
width in 1D and the largest sampled distance from the mapped centre to the
boundary in higher dimensions. The solution scale is
$S_K=|\overline u_K|$ in 1D, with
$\overline u_K=|K|^{-1}\int_Ku_h\,dx$; in higher dimensions $S_K$ is the
largest sampled $|u_h|$ within $K$. Precise geometric sampling and the
small-scale safeguard are given in \cref{sec:kxrcf-realization}.

Let $k$ be the reference solution order, $\tau$ the detector threshold,
and $\mathcal A$ the set of elements selected for damping. The assembled
indicator and selection rule are
\begin{equation}
 I_K=\frac{\left|\displaystyle\int_{\partial K^-}
                  [u_h]_{K,N}\,ds\right|}
                 {r_K^{(k+1)/2}|\partial K^-|S_K},
 \qquad K\in\mathcal A\ \Longleftrightarrow\ I_K>\tau.
 \label{eq:kxrcf-indicator}
\end{equation}
The default threshold is $\tau=1$; $I_K=0$ when there is no inflow measure.
The radius is denoted by $r_K$ here to distinguish it from the OFDG length
$h_K$. The face measure averages the jump, and $S_K$ normalizes the state
magnitude. The absolute value is taken \emph{after} integration, so signed
contributions can cancel. This differs from the absolute-jump integral used
by the OFDG sensor.

For motivation, suppose the averaged signed jump is
$\mathcal O(r_K^{k+1})$ in a smooth region and $S_K=\mathcal O(1)$.
Then $I_K=\mathcal O(r_K^{(k+1)/2})$ tends to zero under refinement.
An unresolved discontinuity with a noncancelling $\mathcal O(1)$ averaged jump
instead gives $I_K=\mathcal O(r_K^{-(k+1)/2})$. These conditional estimates
explain the normalization; they are not a convergence theorem or a guarantee
that every discontinuity is detected. Cancellation and a small local scale
can both affect the decision. The numerical value also depends on the
coordinate scaling through $r_K$, so a threshold must be interpreted with
that scaling specified.

In one dimension, with positive velocity and cell width $\Delta x_j$, the
inflow boundary is the left endpoint and $r_K=\Delta x_j/2$. The implemented
scale is $S_K=|\overline u_K|$, giving
\begin{equation}
 I_j=\frac{|u_h(x_{j-1/2}^{+})-u_h(x_{j-1/2}^{-})|}
                 {(\Delta x_j/2)^{(k+1)/2}|\overline u_j|},
 \label{eq:kxrcf-1d}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is resolved. The relative scale floor in \cref{sec:kxrcf-realization} handles a
vanishing or very small mean.

\subsection{Conservative systems and transport direction}
\label{sec:kxrcf-transport}
For each conservative component $c$, the implementation forms $I_{K,c}$ from
\cref{eq:kxrcf-indicator} and pools the decision:
\begin{equation}
 I_K=\max_c I_{K,c},\qquad
 \mathcal A=\{K:I_K>\tau\}.
 \label{eq:kxrcf-components}
\end{equation}
All components are therefore filtered on the same active set. This pooling,
together with the following transport choices, defines the project-specific
system extension; it should not be mistaken for characteristic-variable
limiting.

For prescribed advection, $v_n$ is the arithmetic mean of the normal velocities
on the two sides. For one-dimensional Burgers flow it is
$v_n=\tfrac12(u_1+u_2)n$. For Euler it is
\begin{equation}
 v_n=\tfrac12(\boldsymbol v_1+\boldsymbol v_2)\cdot\boldsymbol n.
 \label{eq:kxrcf-euler-inflow}
\end{equation}
This is a fluid transport direction, not the Euler spectral radius.
Characteristic fields can travel in both directions at a subsonic interface;
a single signed velocity does not describe them all. OFDG uses the full
one-sided radii $|\boldsymbol v_i\cdot\boldsymbol n|+c_i$ for damping strength,
whereas the sign of \cref{eq:kxrcf-euler-inflow} controls KXRCF inflow only.

\subsection{Physical geometry and numerical realization}
\label{sec:kxrcf-realization}
In higher dimensions, $r_K$ is the largest sampled distance from the mapped
reference centre to mapped vertices and, on curved elements, boundary
quadrature points. It is a sampled geometric scale rather than an exact
circumradius for an arbitrary curved cell. Face quadrature supplies the signed
jump integral and the physical inflow measure. Unit normals and the transport
sign are evaluated at those points. Transport within $128\epsilon$ times the
local speed scale is treated as tangential, where $\epsilon$ is machine
precision.

For component $c$, the scale used in the denominator is
\begin{equation}
 S_{K,c}^{\rm eff}=\max(S_{K,c},10^{-14}S_c^{\max}),
 \qquad S_c^{\max}=\max_K S_{K,c}.
\end{equation}
Globally zero components are skipped.
This avoids division by a vanishing local scale while preserving the
multiplicative scale dependence away from numerical roundoff. Physical
boundary faces are omitted because the indicator uses neighbouring DG traces;
boundary conditions remain part of the DG evolution.

Element and face evaluation matrices, geometric weights, normals, and the
factor $r_K^{(k+1)/2}$ are prepared at construction. Local face transformations
are retained, but solution values and transport directions are reevaluated
on every application. At shared MPI faces, neighbour coefficients are imported
and each rank accumulates only its own element's contribution. Geometry and
solution spaces must remain fixed during the lifetime of the indicator.

\subsection{Coupling to OFDG and limitations}
KXRCF determines $\mathcal A$ from the candidate DG state. An interior face is
needed by the OFDG sensor whenever at least one adjacent element is active;
this ensures that active elements receive all their interface contributions.
The damping map is applied only to active elements. Its mean-preservation
identity therefore survives gating, and inactive coefficients remain unchanged.

KXRCF requires no solution derivatives, although the subsequent OFDG sensor
does. A small active set does not eliminate the cost of cell normalization,
inflow integration, or OFDG derivative preparation. Nor does the indicator
alone guarantee nonoscillatory or positive solutions. Threshold sensitivity,
missed fronts, and unnecessary activation in smooth regions require empirical
assessment in the eventual numerical study.
