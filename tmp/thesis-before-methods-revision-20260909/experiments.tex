\section{Smooth accuracy: what does filtering cost?}
\label{sec:results-convergence-tables}
Periodic 1D advection uses $\Omega=[0,1]$, velocity one, and
$u_0(x)=\sin^2(2\pi x)$. Its exact solution is $u_0((x-t)\bmod1)$.
The implementation uses its standard nodal initialization; the separately
available Gauss--Radau option is not used to claim superconvergence here.
\Cref{tab:smooth-errors} gives the finest errors and the rate on the last
refinement for each method. Complete refinement values are retained in the
machine-readable convergence table.
\input{generated/preview/smooth_table}

DG approaches orders two, three, and four for P1, P2, and P3. On the finest P2
grid, the error of OFDG is 1.648 times the DG error; OFDG--KXRCF is within
0.001\% of DG. This supports selective filtering as a way to retain smooth
accuracy on this case. The P1 gated error remains appreciably larger than DG,
so the P2 observation must not be generalized to every degree. Some filtered
rates exceed $k+1$ on these meshes; this can reflect a changing balance of
filter and discretization error and is not a superconvergence result.

At the finest P3 resolution, halving CFL changes the $L^2$ error by 0.000605\%
for DG, 0.002908\% for OFDG, and 0.000316\% for OFDG--KXRCF. This checks
sensitivity of the reported error norm at that resolution, not temporal
independence of every run or of every solution feature.

For Burgers, $f(u)=u^2/2$ and $u_0(x)=0.5+\sin x$ on the periodic interval
$[0,2\pi]$. The smooth comparison ends at $T=0.6$, before the first shock at
$t=1$. The exact smooth value solves $u=0.5+\sin(x-tu)$.
The finest-grid rates are about three; DG and gated OFDG have identical
reported errors on this sequence (\cref{tab:smooth-errors}).

\section{Transport on curved elements}
Straight and curved tests solve periodic transport on $[0,1]^2$ with velocity
$(0.7,0.3)$ and $u_0(x,y)=\sin^2(\pi(x+y))$. The exact solution translates this
function. Starting from a Cartesian mesh, cubic geometry nodes are displaced by
\begin{equation}
 (x,y)\longmapsto(x+d(x,y),y+d(x,y)),\quad
 d(x,y)=0.04\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2\pi y).
\end{equation}
The displacement is interpolated into cubic geometry; the finite-element map
is therefore polynomial, not the exact trigonometric map. This is a
paper-inspired adaptation for testing the curved implementation, not a native
NURBS experiment or a reproduction of a published curved-mesh benchmark.

\Cref{tab:smooth-errors} separates straight and curved results. On the finest
curved grid, OFDG has 4.715 times the DG error, whereas gated OFDG has 1.002
times the DG error. All three errors decrease over the tested refinements.
These observations complement the operator checks in
\cref{chap:implementation}: they test evolution of a smooth field on a changing
mesh resolution, rather than only isolated matrices. They do not establish
robustness on highly distorted cells or on curved Euler shocks.

\section{Scalar discontinuities: inspect the polynomial, not just the mean}
Piecewise periodic advection follows the data of the 2021 OFDG Example 1(b)~\cite{LuEtAl2021}:
\begin{equation}
 u_0(x)=\begin{cases}
 \sin(2\pi x),&0.3\leq x\leq0.8,\\
 \cos(2\pi x)-0.5,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}\qquad 0\leq x<1.
\end{equation}
The reference at $T=1.1$ is the exact periodic translation. Burgers uses the
same smooth initial data as above but evolves to $T=2.2$, after shock formation.
No exact post-shock reference is asserted for its plotted profile.
\Cref{fig:scalar-shocks} includes a full profile, a narrow discontinuity view,
and a difference panel. The last is necessary because near-coincident curves
can hide differences between the filtered methods.

\begin{figure}[p]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/preview/figures/scalar_shocks.pdf}
\caption{P2 scalar profiles with 256 cells. Left: translated piecewise data at
$T=1.1$, including an exact reference. Right: Burgers at $T=2.2$.
Rows show overview, jump/shock detail, and differences from OFDG. Shading marks
the enlarged interval. Lines join samples only within a cell. Near-zero gated
Burgers differences are quantified in the text.}
\label{fig:scalar-shocks}\end{figure}
\input{generated/preview/extrema_table}

The sampled DG overshoot for piecewise advection is 0.1362 above the initial
maximum; its Burgers undershoot is 0.5415 below the initial minimum.
Filtering substantially reduces these excursions (\cref{tab:scalar-extrema}).
This is empirical suppression, not a maximum-principle guarantee.
Within the plotted jump window, OFDG--KXRCF differs from OFDG by at most
$7.09\times10^{-4}$ for piecewise advection and $4.95\times10^{-4}$ for Burgers.
OEDG's Burgers difference reaches 0.238 and determines the bottom-right vertical
scale; the almost invisible gated curve is expected. A narrower transition
alone is not sufficient evidence of a better solution if it also has larger
oscillations or a shifted shock.

\section{Euler shocks and wave preservation}
All Euler tests use $\gamma=1.4$. The 1D domain is $[-5,5]$ with exterior states fixed to the initial boundary data. Lax begins with primitive states
$(\rho,v,p)=(0.445,0.698,3.528)$ for $x<0$ and $(0.5,0,0.571)$ otherwise.
The Shu--Osher initial state is $(3.857143,2.629369,10.33333)$ for $x<-4$ and
$(1+0.2\sin(5x),0,1)$ elsewhere~\cite{ShuOsher1989}.
These fixed exterior states are supplied to the numerical flux; they are
distinct from the extrapolated outflow condition used in the 2D test.

\begin{figure}[p]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/preview/figures/euler_profiles.pdf}
\caption{Euler density: Lax at $T=1.3$ (left) and Shu--Osher at $T=1.8$
(right). Top and middle rows show P2 profiles and narrow shock views, including
OEDG and the 4,096-cell WENO reference. The Shu--Osher overview is cropped to
$[-1,3]$. Bottom left compares physical cell means for gated OFDG at equal
unknown count, with a local degree legend; bottom right resolves the
shock-generated waves. Every method uses the common positivity treatment.}
\label{fig:euler-profiles}\end{figure}

The equal-unknown Lax comparison uses $N(k+1)=384$ scalar coefficients, or
1,152 conservative coefficients for the three-component Euler system. It changes
both degree and cell width. The cell-mean panel is therefore a practical
resolution comparison, not a formal order experiment; equal unknown count also
does not imply equal runtime.
\input{generated/preview/reference_table}

The Shu--Osher wave window $0.9\leq x\leq1.65$ is more discriminating than the
full-domain shock view. Its sampled density peak-to-trough range is 1.590 for
DG, 1.495 for OFDG, 1.529 for gated OFDG, and 1.383 for OEDG; the fine WENO
range is 1.615. The gated result retains more wave variation than all-cell
OFDG here. This range combines amplitude and phase effects and must not replace
a resolved-reference error analysis. DG's larger variation also comes with
many more positivity interventions: 6,252 cell-limiting events, compared with
3, 3, and 2 respectively. These count events over time, not distinct cells.

\section{A small two-dimensional Euler Riemann case}
The domain is $[0,1]^2$ with extrapolated outflow boundaries (exterior state equal to interior trace). Quadrants split
at $x=y=0.5$ have the following primitive states, a small case from the
Riemann-test tradition~\cite{LaxLiu1998}:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
Quadrant & $\rho$ & $v_x$ & $v_y$ & $p$\\\midrule
Lower left & 0.8 & 0 & 0 & 1\\
Upper left & 1 & 0.7276 & 0 & 1\\
Lower right & 1 & 0 & 0.7276 & 1\\
Upper right & 0.5313 & 0 & 0 & 0.4\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
The test ends at $T=0.25$. The density maps in \cref{fig:riemann-maps} show
physical cell means; the shared range covers every plotted value without
clipping. The cuts in \cref{fig:riemann-cuts} reconstruct each degree-two
polynomial at exactly $y=0.740$, with breaks at interfaces and no cross-cell
smoothing. This makes their narrow shock comparison spatially consistent.

All six runs finish without rejected steps and with positive reported final
sampled density and pressure. DG requires 122 and 482 cell-limiting events on
the two grids; both OFDG variants require none. These are assisted DG results,
not evidence that the unassisted DG scheme remains admissible. At $64^2$ the
maximum difference between gated and all-cell OFDG along the matched cut is
0.02265, small enough to be hard to see in the overview but not zero.
There is no independent 2D reference here. Sharper appearance is insufficient
to decide which method is more accurate.
