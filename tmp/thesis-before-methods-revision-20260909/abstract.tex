\begin{abstract}
This work studies an adapted oscillation-free discontinuous Galerkin (OFDG)
implementation in MFEM for hyperbolic conservation laws. The method damps
nonconstant element content through nested physical $L^2$ projections and can
be restricted by a KXRCF troubled-cell indicator. Its realization operates in
the host coefficient basis and supports static polynomial curvilinear geometry.
Curved derivative sensors use successively projected physical derivatives;
they are not exact higher derivatives of the mapped solution. The verified
support boundary and the MFEM limitation for native NURBS faces are explicit.

A small preliminary study compares DG, all-cell adapted OFDG, and gated OFDG,
with OEDG on selected affine problems. It includes smooth transport, Burgers
flow, Lax and Shu--Osher problems, straight/curved advection, and a small
2D Euler Riemann problem. The 103 solver runs and four independent reference
solves completed in 7.35 minutes on the laptop. Filtering suppresses the large
sampled DG excursions in the scalar shock tests. At the finest P2 transport
grids, gated OFDG has errors close to the unfiltered DG baseline. Wave-detail
and positivity diagnostics expose differences that whole-domain profiles hide.

These observations characterize the tested configurations rather than establish
universal superiority or new convergence theory. Visible placeholders identify
missing detector, reference-accuracy, and cadence evidence needed for stronger
claims. Large-scale performance and broad Euler robustness remain outside the
current empirical conclusions.
\end{abstract}
