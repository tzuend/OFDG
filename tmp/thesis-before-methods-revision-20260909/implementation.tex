\chapter{Implementation and verification}
\label{chap:implementation}
The implementation separates the physical equation, the element geometry, and
the filtering operation. This separation allows the same projection kernel to
serve scalar and Euler equations and lets geometry corrections be tested
without changing the underlying conservation law. MFEM provides meshes, basis
functions, transformations, and distributed ownership~\cite{AndersonEtAl2021}.

\section{Reference data and physical element operators}
\label{sec:curved-construction}
Reference basis evaluations and derivative graphs are reusable by finite
element signature. Geometry-dependent mass, projection, derivative, face-weight,
and normal data belong to each physical curved element or neighbour. Caches
are immutable: changing geometry, topology, or the solution space requires
constructing new filter objects. Mutable scratch arrays mean simultaneous
calls on the same object are unsupported.

The physical matrices are assembled as derived in
\cref{eq:curved-weighted-masses,eq:curved-derivative-assembly}. Reference
projectors are reused only when the constant affine Jacobian weight cancels.
Curved projectors and derivative matrices belong to the physical element.

Curved quadrature uses order $2k+2\max(0,\mathrm{OrderW})+8$, where MFEM's
\texttt{OrderW} estimates the transformation-weight order. Positive tensor/Duffy
rules and symmetric triangular-face sampling avoid cancellation and
orientation-dependent samples. Physical face measure normalizes jump integrals;
physical normals are evaluated at those same face points. This is a practical
quadrature policy, supported by sensitivity checks rather than a universal
exactness claim for arbitrary geometry.

\section{Physics, time integration, and MPI}
MPI (Message Passing Interface) distributes the mesh across processes called
ranks, each owning part of the domain.
A face-physics interface returns two nonnegative normal radii for damping and
one signed transport velocity for KXRCF. Adapted OFDG uses the face traces;
OEDG's state-dependent radii use the adjacent cell means. Their different
choices are deliberate and recorded in the method contract.

An ordinary local face updates both adjacent elements. A shared MPI face
updates only the element owned by that rank after exchanging neighbour
coefficients; the neighbour rank performs the complementary update. Global
amplitudes use collective reductions. Local and neighbour geometry follows the
same construction, so an MPI boundary is not a physical boundary.

The common method/cadence interface selects post-step DG/OFDG and stage-wise
OEDG by default. A small RK callback driver also applies the common Euler
positivity checks. Rejected candidates leave the accepted state and time
unchanged. Post-step OFDG is the configuration studied here; it is not justified
by an unsupported claim that stage filtering never improves accuracy.

The existing curved validation represents $\sin x$ at P4 on a smoothly deformed
square. Its physical $L^2$ derivative errors are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
Cells / direction & first derivative & second & third\\\midrule
2 & $1.375\,10^{-5}$ & $5.498\,10^{-4}$ & $1.384\,10^{-2}$\\
4 & $8.703\,10^{-7}$ & $6.940\,10^{-5}$ & $3.463\,10^{-3}$\\
8 & $5.461\,10^{-8}$ & $8.699\,10^{-6}$ & $8.661\,10^{-4}$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These approach orders four, three, and two. They validate this manufactured
case, not exactness of projected higher derivatives. The first derivative is
also compared with an independently assembled weak projection. Increasing the
quadrature order by eight leaves the tested derivative matrix within tolerance.

The retained local verification record reports passing release, sanitizer, and
curved checks. It is documented in \path{docs/curved-validation.md}, with logs
under \path{build/curved-validation/}. Tests shared by OFDG and OEDG cannot make
their comparison an independent verification of every shared component;
analytic identities and separately assembled checks provide that additional
reference. No new solver run was required for the report revision.
