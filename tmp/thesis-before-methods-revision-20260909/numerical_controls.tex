\section{Common numerical controls}
\label{sec:numerical-controls}
\subsection{Euler positivity is separate from oscillation damping}
An oscillation filter need not prevent negative density or pressure. The common
positivity limiter instead scales each local state toward its conservative
cell mean. It constrains density first and pressure by bisection along the same
line segment, following the mean-preserving scaling principle
of~\cite{ZhangShu2010}. All components use the same scaling so that the
conservative means are preserved.

Checks use volume nodes and the local/shared face evaluation points required
by the evolution and filter operators. A nonphysical mean cannot be repaired
by scaling toward itself; the candidate step is rejected and retried with a
smaller time step, subject to a retry limit. The preview enables this treatment
for every Euler method, including DG, and records its interventions. Thus
``DG'' in an Euler plot means DG without an oscillation filter but with the
common positivity control.

\subsection{Independent one-dimensional references}
The finite-volume reference solver uses component-wise WENO5 reconstruction,
Lax--Friedrichs flux splitting, and SSPRK3~\cite{JiangShu1996,GottliebShu1998}.
It shares no DG projector, detector, or damping code. Smooth Euler and exact
Sod tests check its implementation~\cite{Sod1978}. The preview compares
2,048- and 4,096-cell references; fine densities are averaged in pairs onto the
coarse grid. A reference may be useful for viewing a profile while still being
insufficient for a quantitative error claim near a shock. That distinction is
carried into the results and missing-work register.
