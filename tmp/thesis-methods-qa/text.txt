Principal findings and the final conclusion, after the numerical ex-

High-order discontinuous Galerkin methods provide local approxima-
tions of solutions to hyperbolic conservation laws, but unresolved dis-
continuities can produce spurious oscillations. This thesis investigates
projection-based oscillation damping and its implementation on static
curvilinear meshes. The approach combines an adapted oscillation-free
discontinuous Galerkin (OFDG) filter with optional KXRCF troubled-
cell indication, separating the strength of damping from the decision
of where to apply it.
The formulation acts on finite element functions independently of their
coefficient basis. PhysicalL 2 projections separate nonconstant compo-
nents for damping while retaining cell averages. On curved elements,
first physical derivatives are projected into the solution space and ap-
plied recursively to construct the derivative sensor. These operations
represent successively projected derivatives rather than exact higher
physical derivatives. The methodological account distinguishes pub-
lished formulations from the adaptations used here and specifies the
geometric and numerical assumptions needed for their realization in
MFEM.

Chapter 1
Introduction
1.1 Motivation
Hyperbolic conservation laws describe the transport of quantities such as
mass, momentum, and energy. Their solutions can contain both smooth
structures and sharp fronts. Even smooth initial data can develop a discon-
tinuity, so a numerical method must accommodate qualitatively different
behaviour within the same calculation.
Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods combine finite element approxima-
tion within each mesh element with numerical fluxes between elements.
Independent local polynomial representations allow high-order approxima-
tion without imposing continuity across interfaces [1]. Near an unresolved
front, however, a polynomial approximation can develop overshoots and
undershoots. Damping these oscillations indiscriminately can also remove
smooth features that a high-order method is intended to resolve.
Projection-based damping offers a way to separate these concerns. The cell
mean can be retained while nonconstant parts of the approximation are at-
tenuated. The scalar OFDG formulation of Lu, Liu, and Shu [3] and its
extension to hyperbolic systems [2] provide the starting point. The OEDG
formulation of Peng, Sun, and Wu [4] further motivates normalized sensing
and exact integration of a frozen damping step. A separate troubled-cell
indicator can restrict the operation to selected cells. The central issue is
then how to define the damping, its sensor, and its geometric realization
consistently.
1.2 Aim and research questions
This thesis concerns a basis-independent realization of an adapted OFDG
method in the finite element library MFEM. Here, basis independence

The work is guided by three questions:

3. How do damping and selective activation affect accuracy and resolu-

The first two questions determine the mathematical construction and its
implementation. The third requires an agreed set of numerical experiments;
its answer is deliberately left open in this draft.
1.3 Scope and approach
The construction uses a fixed mesh and a uniform solution order. It includes
scalar conservation laws and conservative systems, with compressible Euler
providing the system formulation. Static polynomial curved element maps
are considered; moving meshes and variable solution order are outside the
scope. On curved elements, differentiation generally leaves the mapped ap-
proximation space. The method therefore projects first physical derivatives
back into that space and applies these projected operators recursively. The
distinction between this construction and exact higher differentiation is cen-
tral to the curvilinear extension.
The thesis separates three kinds of statements: identities derived from pro-
jection algebra, implementation choices defining the actual method, and em-
pirical conclusions requiring numerical evidence. In particular, cell-mean
preservation by the filter does not by itself establish positivity, full-scheme
stability, or accuracy near a shock. Published analyses are used under their
stated assumptions, not transferred automatically to the adapted method.
1.4 Organisation of the thesis
Chapter 2 introduces the conservation-law and DG notation for a reader
familiar with finite elements. Chapter 3 develops the adapted OFDG formu-
lation, KXRCF gating, the curvilinear construction, and the related OEDG
implementation. It also states numerical controls and essential implementa-
tion assumptions. Coefficient-space details appear in Chapter A.

This chapter assumes familiarity with the finite element idea: approximate
a function in a finite-dimensional space and require a residual to vanish
against test functions. DG retains that idea but removes continuity between
elements. The price is that communication across each interface must be
defined explicitly. We first explain this communication, then the time update
that the filters modify. The introductory DG treatment follows the finite
element viewpoint of [1].

Chapter 3
Methods
This chapter defines the numerical method used in this work. The DG for-
mulation in Chapter 2 supplies the conservative evolution. Adapted OFDG
adds local damping, and KXRCF optionally determines which cells receive
it. The curvilinear construction gives these operations their physical mean-
ing on mapped elements. OEDG is described as a related method avail-
able for comparison, followed by numerical controls and implementation
assumptions.
3.1 Baseline discretization and method composition
The baseline is the weak DG formulation Eq. (2.4) with the Rusanov flux
Eq. (2.5). It uses a fixed scalar DG space, repeated for each conservative
component, and explicit Runge–Kutta time integration. Periodic boundaries
identify the corresponding traces; physical boundaries supply an exterior
state through the DG flux. The oscillation sensor uses interior interfaces and
does not replace this boundary treatment.
For a post-step filter, one update has the form
U∗ =H ∆t(Un),U n+1 =F ∆t(U∗), (3.1)
whereHis the RKDG update andFis the frozen damping map defined
below. With KXRCF, the active set is computed fromU ∗ before applyingF.
Without an oscillation filter,Fis the identity. The implementation defaults
to post-step OFDG and stage-wise OEDG; the location of filtering is part of
the numerical method, not an incidental software setting. Euler additionally
uses the admissibility control in Section 3.6.

3. Methods
3.2 Adapted OFDG: damping without changing the cell
mean
The DG state from Chapter 2 may contain unresolved oscillations. Adapted
OFDG reduces nonconstant content within each element while preserving
its mean. Three ingredients determine the operation: nested projections
separate orders of variation, interelement jumps measure roughness, and
wave-speed-scaled rates determine how strongly each part is damped.
3.2.1 Relation to the published methods
The nameadapted OFDGis used throughout this report for the specific hy-
brid construction implemented here. Projection damping originates in [3, 2];
global scale normalization and exponential decay are motivated by [4]. The
earlier multidimensional OFDG sensors evaluate vertex jumps, and the sys-
tem formulation uses characteristic variables. Here we average absolute
jumps over faces and form separate rates for physical conservative compo-
nents. KXRCF gating is optional.
These choices define an implementation variant, not a literal reproduction
of one paper. The conservation and damping identities below follow from
projection algebra. Published convergence theorems for different sensors are
not a proof for this variant, particularly on curved elements with projected
derivatives.

3.7 Implementation assumptions and geometric scope
The implementation separates equation-dependent face physics, geometry-
dependent operators, and damping. MFEM supplies meshes, finite element
bases, geometric transformations, and distributed ownership. This organi-
sation allows the same projection algebra to act on scalar fields and conser-
vative systems.
The supported construction assumes full-dimensional elements, a fixed
mesh, and uniform solution orderk≥1. It covers affine intervals and
polynomial curved triangles, quadrilaterals, tetrahedra, hexahedra, and
prisms. Different supported element families may coexist at the same order.
Moving meshes, variable solution order, pyramids, and embedded surfaces
are excluded.
Native NURBS geometry is not supported by the two-sided face path used
with the project’s pinned MFEM version. A NURBS mesh may instead be
explicitly approximated by polynomial geometry before the filter is con-
structed. This changes the geometric representation and is separate from
the solution-spaceL 2 projection derived above. It does not imply that MFEM
lacks NURBS support in general. The OEDG implementation is treated here
as an affine comparator; the curvilinear extension described in this chapter
concerns adapted OFDG and KXRCF.
3.7.1 Reference data and physical operators
Reference basis evaluations and the derivative graph are reusable by finite
element signature. Physical mass, projection, and derivative matrices belong
to each curved element. The reference projector is sufficient only when the
constant affine Jacobian weight cancels. Element and face geometry data
remain fixed during the lifetime of a filter; a change in mesh geometry,
topology, or solution space requires reconstruction.
Curved integration uses quadrature order 2k+2 max(0, OrderW) +8, where
MFEM’sOrderWestimates the transformation-weight order. Positive tensor
or Duffy quadrature and symmetric triangular sampling are used to reduce
orientation-dependent sampling effects. This is an integration policy, not
a guarantee of exact integration for every mapped function. The physical
projection identities refer to the resulting quadrature inner product unless
exact integration is stated explicitly.

The implementation retains derivative coefficient states for every local cell
and rebuilds them for each new solution. For gated filtering, local faces
whose two neighbours are inactive are skipped before their geometry is
constructed. Adjacent damping shells reuse their shared projection. These
choices eliminate redundant work without changing the operator. The de-
tailed matrix representation is given in Chapter A.
3.7.2 Distributed evaluation
MPI distributes elements across processes, or ranks. An ordinary interior
face contributes to both adjacent elements. At a shared face, a rank imports
neighbour coefficients and accumulates only its own element’s contribution;
the adjacent rank performs the complementary operation. Global solution
amplitudes require collective reductions. Local and imported elements use
the same physical operator construction, so an MPI interface has no addi-
tional physical boundary condition.
Geometry caches are fixed, but their integration-point state and the working
arrays are mutable. Concurrent applications of the same filter object are
therefore unsupported. This ownership convention keeps numerical state
separate from reusable geometric data.
3.7.3 Verification principles
The algebra suggests independent checks: constants and component-wise
cell means should be preserved; inactive cells should remain unchanged;
and a change of coefficient basis should represent the same filtered function.
Curved first derivatives can be compared with an independently assembled
physical projection. Recursive higher derivatives require manufactured-
solution refinement studies, since their definition alone supplies no con-
vergence rate. Quadrature sensitivity, geometric parameterization, and se-
rial/MPI agreement are separate checks of the geometric realization.
These checks distinguish consistency of an implementation from the accu-
racy and robustness of a complete simulation. Their selected numerical
evidence, along with comparisons between methods, belongs in the Results
chapter once the experimental programme has been agreed.

A.1 Scope and geometric assumptions
The implementation is a small compiled MFEM library. It assumes that
the finite element space is nonempty and discontinuous, thatk≥1, and
that all elements have the same order and dimension. Geometry and lo-
cal degree-of- freedom counts may differ. Element maps may be affine or
static polynomial curvilinear, and the mesh must remain unchanged during
the lifetime of the OFDG object. The space and cache-lifetime conditions
are caller responsibilities. Native NURBS geometry is explicitly rejected be-
fore the unsupported face path is entered. The public header exposes the
OFDG constructors, stabilization, and decay only. KXRCF is an indepen-
dent optional method and is included explicitly when the activation mask is
required.
Different elements need not have the same Jacobian. They may have dif-
ferent sizes, reference geometries, and local coefficient counts, and may be
translated, rotated, or affinely skewed. An immutable repository constructs
the required projection and derivative operators once per finite-element
signature. Non-affine elements additionally own physical projection and
projected-derivative matrices assembled with geometry-dependent quadra-
ture. Native NURBS geometry is rejected: the pinned MFEM implementa-
tion does not support the two-sided NURBS face transformations required
here. Projecting such a mesh to polynomial nodes changes the rational ge-
ometry approximately and is therefore a distinct supported case.

dard reference element. The affine assumption means that this map has the

A.5 Runtime sequence and storage policy
For every input state, the implementation performs the following opera-
tions:
1. evaluate the global mean and scale (3.8) at volume quadrature points;
2. rebuild and store all derivative coefficient states on every element;
3. loop over MFEM interior faces, evaluate the normal wave speeds and
the mean absolute jump measures (3.7), and accumulate the rates (3.9)
on both neighboring elements;
4. apply either the coefficient-space damping operator (3.10) or the exact
decay map (3.13).
